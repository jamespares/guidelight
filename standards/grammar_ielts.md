# ELT Grammar Inventory (CEFR A1–C2) & Official IELTS↔CEFR Mapping
*Anchored to English Grammar Profile (EGP), British Council/EAQUALS Core Inventory for General English (North, Ortega & Sheehan 2010), Cambridge exam handbooks, IELTS public band descriptors, Cambridge/IELTS CEFR alignment research (Taylor 2004; SiLT Vol. 26).*

## PART A — GRAMMAR INVENTORY (point | can-do descriptor | example)

### A1
1. Present simple of be | Identify people, states, jobs | She is a teacher.
2. have got | Express possession | I've got two sisters.
3. Imperatives (+/-) | Give instructions/commands | Open your books. Don't run.
4. there is / there are | State existence/location | There's a bank near here.
5. can (ability) | Say what one is able to do | I can swim.
6. Present simple (1st/2nd person) | Describe habits/routines/facts | We live in Madrid.
7. Present simple 3rd-person -s | Agreement in routines | He works in a bank.
8. Present continuous | Actions in progress now | She's cooking dinner.
9. Yes/no questions + short answers | Ask for confirmation | Do you like tea? — Yes, I do.
10. Wh- questions (what/where/who/when) | Seek basic information | Where do you live?
11. How + adjective questions | Ask about age/size/price | How old are you?
12. Article a/an | Introduce singular referents | I saw a dog.
13. Definite the (basic) | Refer to known/unique things | The sun is hot.
14. Regular plurals -s/-es | Pluralise nouns | two buses, three cats
15. Demonstratives this/that/these/those | Point to near/far items | This is my pen.
16. Possessive adjectives (my/your/his…) | Mark ownership | Her name is Ana.
17. Possessive 's | Relate people to things/people | Maria's car is red.
18. Subject & object pronouns | Refer back to people/things | I like him. She knows me.
19. Prepositions of place (in/on/at/under/next to) | Locate objects | The cat is on the chair.
20. Prepositions of time (at/in/on) | Fix times/days/months | at six, on Monday, in July
21. Adjectives before nouns | Describe things simply | a big house, a nice day
22. Adverbs of frequency (always/usually/never) | Say how often things happen | I never drink coffee.
23. very / really | Intensify adjectives | It's very cold today.
24. Conjunctions and/but/or | Link words and simple clauses | I like tea but not coffee.
25. Negatives: be/do + not | Negate simple statements | I'm not tired. I don't smoke.
26. like/love/hate + -ing | Express preferences | I like swimming.
27. some/any (basic) | Refer to indefinite quantity | There are some eggs. There isn't any milk.
28. would like ('d like) | Make polite requests | I'd like a coffee, please.
29. Let's + infinitive | Make suggestions | Let's go home.
30. How much/many (basic) | Ask about quantity/price | How much is it?

### A2
1. Past simple: regular verbs | Narrate completed events | I watched a film last night.
2. Past simple: high-frequency irregulars | Narrate past with went/had/took | We went to Rome in May.
3. Past of be (was/were) | Describe past states | It was cold yesterday.
4. Past simple negatives/questions (did) | Ask/negate about the past | Did you see it? I didn't go.
5. Past continuous | Ongoing past action, often interrupted | I was sleeping when you called.
6. be going to (plans/intentions) | Talk about future plans | I'm going to visit her on Friday.
7. Present continuous for future arrangements | Confirm fixed arrangements | We're meeting at seven.
8. will/won't (instant decisions, predictions) | Decide/offer/predict on the spot | I'll help you with that.
9. Comparatives (-er/more … than) | Compare two things | My car is faster than yours.
10. Superlatives (-est/most … in/of) | Identify extremes in a group | It's the tallest building in town.
11. Countable vs uncountable nouns | Distinguish count/mass reference | a few apples / some milk
12. Quantifiers much/many/a lot of | Express large quantity | We don't have much time.
13. (a) few / (a) little | Express small quantity | Add a little sugar.
14. too / (not) enough | Express excess or sufficiency | It's too hot; I'm not tall enough.
15. must/mustn't | State rules and strong obligation | You must stop at the light.
16. have to / don't have to | External obligation/necessity | I have to wear a uniform.
17. should/shouldn't (advice) | Give advice | You should see a doctor.
18. can/can't (permission/rules) | Grant/refuse permission | You can park here.
19. could (past ability) | Describe past abilities | I could swim when I was five.
20. Adverbs of manner (-ly) | Describe how actions happen | She drives carefully.
21. Adverbs of degree (quite/really/too) | Modify adjectives/adverbs | It's quite good.
22. Possessive pronouns (mine/yours/his…) | Avoid noun repetition | The red one is mine.
23. Reflexive pronouns (myself/yourself…) | Refer back to the subject | He hurt himself.
24. Gerund after verbs of preference | Talk about enjoyed activities | I enjoy reading.
25. Infinitive of purpose | Explain why we do things | I went to the shop to buy milk.
26. would like + to-infinitive | Express wants politely | I'd like to go home.
27. Prepositions of movement (to/into/out of/through) | Describe direction | She walked into the room.
28. Conjunctions because/so/when/while | Link reason, result, time | I stayed home because it rained.
29. Indefinite compounds (something/anything/nothing) | Refer to indefinite things/people | I didn't see anything.
30. Time adverbials (ago/last/yesterday) | Locate events in past time | He left two days ago.

### B1
1. Present perfect: experience (ever/never) | Talk about life experiences | Have you ever been to Japan?
2. Present perfect: just/yet/already | Report recent news | I've just finished.
3. Present perfect: for/since | Describe duration up to now | I've lived here since 2010.
4. Present perfect vs past simple | Contrast finished/unfinished time | I saw it yesterday. / I've seen it already.
5. First conditional | Discuss real future possibilities | If it rains, we'll stay home.
6. Second conditional | Discuss hypothetical present/future | If I had money, I'd travel.
7. Zero conditional | State general truths | If you heat ice, it melts.
8. Passive: present simple | Focus on process, not agent | English is spoken here.
9. Passive: past simple | Report agentless past events | The bridge was built in 1900.
10. Reported speech: statements (backshift) | Report what someone said | He said (that) he was tired.
11. Defining relative clauses (who/which/that) | Identify which person/thing | The man who lives next door is a teacher.
12. Object relative pronoun omission | Build concise relatives | The film (that) I saw was great.
13. used to (past habits/states) | Describe discontinued past | I used to play football.
14. Past perfect (basic sequencing) | Order two past events | When I arrived, the train had left.
15. might/may/could (present speculation) | Express uncertainty | It might rain later.
16. must/can't (deduction) | Draw logical conclusions | You must be tired.
17. ought to | Give recommendations | You ought to rest more.
18. don't have to / needn't | State lack of necessity | You don't have to come if you're busy.
19. Question tags | Check information; keep conversation going | It's hot today, isn't it?
20. Verb patterns: want/decide/hope + to-inf | Choose infinitive patterns | I decided to leave early.
21. Verb patterns: enjoy/mind/finish + -ing | Choose gerund patterns | I don't mind waiting.
22. Gerund as subject | Generalise about activities | Smoking is bad for you.
23. too + adj + to-inf / adj + enough + to-inf | Express consequence | I'm too tired to go out.
24. Common phrasal verbs (sep./insep.) | Use idiomatic verb-particle combos | put off a meeting, look after children
25. Concession: although/though | Concede a contrasting point | Although it rained, we went out.
26. both/neither/either (of) | Refer to two items | Neither answer is right.
27. So do I / Neither do I | Agree without repetition | "I like jazz." — "So do I."
28. Future time clauses (when/before/after/as soon as + present) | Sequence future events | I'll call you when I arrive.
29. Zero article for generic plural nouns | Make generalisations | Dogs are friendly animals.
30. Polite indirect starters (Could you tell me…?) | Soften information requests | Could you tell me where the station is?

### B2
1. Present perfect continuous | Duration/results of ongoing activity | I've been working all day.
2. Past perfect continuous | Duration before a past reference point | She'd been waiting for hours when he arrived.
3. Third conditional | Speculate about unreal pasts | If I'd known, I would have come.
4. Passive: continuous forms | Report ongoing passive events | The road is being repaired.
5. Passive: perfect forms | Report completed passive events | The work has been finished.
6. Passive with modal verbs | Passivise obligation/possibility | It can be done today.
7. wish / if only + past simple | Express present desires/regrets | I wish I lived by the sea.
8. wish / if only + past perfect | Express past regrets | If only I had studied harder.
9. used to vs would (past habits) | Narrate repeated past actions | We would go swimming every summer.
10. be/get used to + -ing | Describe familiarity/adaptation | I'm used to getting up early.
11. Non-defining relative clauses | Add non-essential information | My brother, who lives in Spain, is visiting.
12. Relative clauses with prepositions (formal) | Formal relative constructions | The person to whom I spoke was helpful.
13. Reported questions | Report questions with statement order | She asked me where I lived.
14. Reported orders/requests (tell/ask + to-inf) | Report commands/requests | He told me to sit down.
15. Reporting verbs + pattern (suggest/deny/promise) | Report with nuance | She denied taking the money.
16. Past speculation: could/might/may have + pp | Speculate about the past | He might have missed the bus.
17. Past deduction: must/can't have + pp | Deduce about the past | It can't have been him.
18. should/ought to have + pp | Criticise past (in)action | You should have told me.
19. Future continuous | Action in progress at a future point | This time tomorrow I'll be flying.
20. Future perfect | Completion before a future point | I'll have finished by Friday.
21. Conditional conjunctions (unless/provided (that)/as long as) | Frame conditions precisely | I'll go as long as you come too.
22. Gerund vs infinitive: meaning change (stop/remember/try) | Signal meaning via pattern choice | I stopped smoking. / I stopped to smoke.
23. Causative have/get something done | Describe arranged services | I had my car repaired.
24. Embedded/indirect questions | Ask formally and politely | Do you know what time it starts?
25. Contrast linkers (despite/in spite of/whereas) | Contrast clauses and NPs | Despite the rain, we went out.
26. Result linkers (therefore/as a result/consequently) | Build text-level cohesion | It rained; as a result, the match was cancelled.
27. Correlative conjunctions (neither…nor / either…or / not only…but also) | Coordinate paired alternatives | Neither John nor Mary came.
28. Graded comparison ((not) as…as; the more…the more) | Compare proportionally | The sooner, the better.
29. Verbs of perception + do/doing | Report perceived events | I saw him crossing the road.
30. the + adjective (generic groups) | Generalise about social groups | The unemployed need more support.

### C1
1. Inversion after negative adverbials | Emphasise formally | Never have I seen such chaos.
2. Inversion after only + time/condition | Restrict and emphasise | Only then did I understand.
3. Little did… (negative-verb inversion) | Dramatise unawareness | Little did I know what awaited me.
4. No sooner/hardly/scarcely… than/when | Mark immediate sequence | Hardly had we arrived when it started to rain.
5. Not only… but also (inversion) | Correlative emphasis | Not only did he apologise, he also refunded us.
6. So/such + inversion | Emphasise degree + result | So intense was the heat that we stayed inside.
7. Mixed conditionals | Link past condition to present result | If I'd saved more, I'd be rich now.
8. Inverted conditionals (Had/Were/Should + subject) | Conditional without if | Had I known, I'd have acted differently.
9. If it weren't/hadn't been for | Identify decisive conditions | If it hadn't been for you, I'd have failed.
10. It-cleft sentences | Focus one sentence element | It was John who called.
11. Pseudo-cleft (what-cleft) | Thematise information | What I need is a holiday.
12. Impersonal passive reporting (It is said that…) | Report claims with distance | It is believed that he left the country.
13. Personal passive reporting (is said to + inf) | Attribute claims to subjects | She is thought to be the best candidate.
14. needn't have + pp vs didn't need to | Unnecessary past actions | You needn't have cooked — we'd already eaten.
15. should/might/could have (criticism/reproach) | Evaluate past choices | You might have told me!
16. Modal + continuous infinitive (must be doing) | Speculate about ongoing events | He must be working late.
17. Participle clauses (present/perfect) | Combine clauses concisely | Having finished her work, she left early.
18. Reduced relative clauses | Replace relative with participle | Anyone wanting a ticket should apply early.
19. Passive gerund (being done / having been done) | Layer voice on non-finite forms | He resents being told what to do.
20. whatever/whoever/whichever clauses | Express free choice | Whatever you decide is fine with me.
21. Unreal past: It's (high) time + past | Urge overdue action | It's high time we left.
22. would rather + subject + past | State preferences about others' actions | I'd rather you didn't smoke in here.
23. Future in the past (was going to / was to) | Report past perspective on later events | I was going to call, but I forgot.
24. Narrative tense interplay | Weave past simple/continuous/perfect | The sun was shining; it had been a perfect morning.
25. Formal discourse markers (moreover, nevertheless, consequently) | Organise academic argument | The cost is high; nevertheless, it is justified.
26. Complex prepositional phrases (in terms of, with regard to, on behalf of) | Frame formal relations | With regard to your query of 5 May…
27. Ellipsis & substitution (I hope so; do so) | Avoid repetition cohesively | He promised to help, and he did so.
28. Reporting verbs + -ing (accuse…of, insist on, blame…for) | Sophisticated reporting | She insisted on paying for dinner.
29. Complex comparison (by far / nothing like / not nearly as) | Grade comparisons precisely | This is by far the best option.
30. Advanced quantifier nuance (the whole, every single, all but) | Fine-tune scope | All but three students passed.

### C2
1. Mandative subjunctive (that + base form) | Make formal demands/recommendations | They demanded that he resign immediately.
2. Fixed subjunctive formulae (be that as it may, suffice it to say) | Deploy fossilised subjunctives | Be that as it may, the fact remains.
3. Fronted negative/restrictive inversion (Under no circumstances, On no account) | Control register and emphasis | Under no circumstances are you to leave the building.
4. Concessive inversion with as/though (adj/adv fronting) | Concede emphatically | Strange as it may seem, it's true.
5. Concession with much as | Strong concession with volition verbs | Much as I admire her work, she's wrong here.
6. Concession with albeit | Concede concisely (adverbial) | It was a successful, albeit brief, experiment.
7. However + adjective/adverb concession | Concede any degree | However hard he tried, he couldn't open it.
8. Nominalisation (verb/adjective → noun phrases) | Write concise academic prose | The rapid implementation of the reforms provoked debate.
9. Lexical hedging (would suggest, tend to, appear/seem to) | Make tentative claims | The data would appear to support the hypothesis.
10. Impersonal stance frames (It could be argued that…) | Hedge via impersonalisation | It could be argued that the policy failed.
11. Perfect passive reporting (is said to have done) | Report with past reference | He is rumoured to have resigned.
12. Ellipsis in adverbial clauses (when/while/if + adj/pp) | Economise in formal writing | When in doubt, consult the manual.
13. Advanced ellipsis: sluicing/stripping | Omit recoverable material | He wants to resign, though I don't know why.
14. Archaic/purpose lest | Negative purpose (formal/literary) | He left early lest he be recognised.
15. Counterfactual frames: but for / were it not for | Formal counterfactual conditions | But for your help, we would have failed.
16. Cleft variants: all-cleft / reversed pseudo-cleft | Flexible information focus | All I want is some peace.
17. Object/complement fronting (topicalisation) | Manage discourse topics | That I cannot accept.
18. Absolute (nominative) constructions | Literary adjunct clauses | The meeting over, everyone drifted home.
19. Inversion after place adverbials | Descriptive/literary staging | On the hill stood a lonely house.
20. Not a + noun inversion (emphatic negation) | Rhetorical negation | Not a word did he say.
21. Modal idiom: may well / might (just) as well | Nuanced likelihood/resignation | It may well rain; we might as well stay.
22. Literary wish: would that + clause | Archaic expression of desire | Would that it were true!
23. Conditional frames: supposing/assuming (that) | Tentative hypothetical framing | Supposing he refuses — what then?
24. Complex noun phrases: stacked pre-/post-modification | Dense academic reference | a recently published, peer-reviewed longitudinal study
25. Stance adverbials (arguably, purportedly, allegedly) | Signal evidentiality/attitude | He allegedly falsified the data.
26. Double genitive | Nuanced possession/association | an old friend of mine
27. there + verbs other than be (there exist, there remain) | Formal existential statements | There remains one unresolved problem.
28. Extraposition with gerund/infinitive subjects | Manage heavy subjects | It was no use crying over spilt milk.
29. Determiner minimisers (what little, the few) | Emphasise scarcity | What little money we had was stolen.
30. Colloquial ellipsis (situational subject/aux drop) | Register-appropriate economy | Want a lift? Seen my keys?

## PART B — IELTS ↔ CEFR MAPPING

### B.1 Official band score ↔ CEFR alignment (IELTS partners, ielts.org)
| IELTS overall band | CEFR | IELTS label |
| 8.5–9.0 | C2 | Very Good / Expert user |
| 7.0–8.0 | C1 | Good / Very Good user |
| 5.5–6.5 | B2 | Modest / Competent user |
| 4.0–5.0 | B1 | Limited / Modest user |
| 3.0–3.5 | ≈ A2 (indicative only) | Extremely Limited user |
| ≤ 2.5 | not CEFR-classified (≈ A1 and below, indicative) | Intermittent / Non-user |
- Alignment published for overall band scores; IELTS "is not a level-based test"; scales "overlap rather than match" (IELTS Guide for Teachers).
- Below band 4.0 no formal CEFR classification (effective floor); A2 ≈ 3.0–3.5 indicative extrapolation only.
- Historical research alignment (Taylor 2004; SiLT 26 Fig. 7.3): C2 = 7.5+; C1 = 6.5/7.0; B2 = 5.0–6.0; B1 = 3.5–4.5; A2 = 3.0 — later revised upward to current table.
- Cambridge scale cross-check: C1 Advanced grade C (180–192) ≈ IELTS 6.5–7.0; B2 First 160–172 ≈ IELTS 5.5–6.0.
Sources: ielts.org guide-to-ielts-scores-2025; IELTS Guide for Teachers; ielts.idp.com IELTS and CEFR levels; cambridgeenglish.org Comparing-scores-to-IELTS; SiLT Vol. 26.

### B.2 IELTS public band descriptors (summaries)
Speaking (FC/LR/GRA/P):
- Band 9: full fluency, rare repetition/self-correction; fully coherent; precise idiomatic lexis; full structural range, consistent accuracy; effortless intelligible pronunciation.
- Band 8: fluent, occasional content-related hesitation only; wide LR with skilful idiomatic items and paraphrase; wide range, majority error-free; easy to understand throughout.
- Band 7: speaks at length without noticeable effort despite some hesitation; flexible connectives; some less-common lexis with effective paraphrase; frequent error-free complex sentences though errors persist.
- Band 6: willing to speak at length, hesitation/repetition may lose coherence; lexis wide enough for topics at length; mixes simple/complex with limited flexibility, errors rarely impede.
- Band 5: usually maintains flow via repetition/self-correction and slow speech; over-uses certain connectives; limited lexical flexibility, frequent basic errors; simple sentences accurate, complex attempts error-prone.
- Band 4: noticeable pauses, slow, frequent repetition; basic connectives only; basic vocabulary with frequent errors; basic forms with errors predominating; limited pronunciation.
Writing Task 2 (TR/CC/LR/GRA):
- Band 9: fully addresses all parts, well-developed position; cohesion attracts no attention; wide natural sophisticated LR; full flexibility/accuracy, rare slips.
- Band 8: sufficiently addresses all parts; well-developed relevant position; logical sequencing, well-managed cohesion; wide fluent LR, skilful uncommon items; majority error-free.
- Band 7: addresses all parts, clear position throughout; clear progression with range of cohesive devices (may over/under-use); sufficient LR, some less-common items; variety of complex structures, frequent error-free sentences.
- Band 6: addresses all parts (some more fully); relevant position though conclusions may be unclear; coherent arrangement but mechanical cohesion; adequate LR with some errors; mixes forms, errors rarely impede.
- Band 5: addresses task only partially, limited development; organisation evident but inadequate/over-used connectives; limited LR, noticeable errors causing strain; limited range, frequent errors.
- Band 4: responds minimally/tangentially; position unclear; ideas not arranged coherently; basic LR; very limited range, errors predominate and may distort meaning.

### B.3 Caveats
1. Floor effect: IELTS measures B1–C2; A1–A2 diagnostic items must be anchored to EGP/Core Inventory, not IELTS.
2. Overall-band basis: alignment applies to overall band; skill bands may vary ±1 (productive often lag receptive).
3. Non-equivalence: scales overlap rather than match (Taylor 2004); alignment revised historically.
4. Borderline bands: 8.0 (C1/C2 border), 6.5 (B2/C1 border per institutional policy) — dual-level candidates.
5. Half-band granularity: CEFR level spans ~3 half-bands; many-to-one mapping.
6. Rounding: overall = mean of 4 skills, .25/.75 round up (IELTS Guide for Teachers).
